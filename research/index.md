# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-microscope" %}Research

Our research sits at the intersection of cellular biology and population
science. We believe the most durable insights come from studying mechanisms
in controlled systems and then stress-testing those findings against the
complexity of real human diversity. That same principle guides how we train
researchers: rigorous methods, scaffolded entry points, and explicit attention
to who gets access to research in the first place.

## Population-Scale Analysis with All of Us

The [All of Us Research Program](https://allofus.nih.gov/) provides
longitudinal health, genomic, and survey data from a nationally diverse
cohort. We leverage this resource to ask whether cellular mechanisms
identified at the bench are reflected in population-level patterns and to
examine how social determinants of health modulate biological outcomes.

Our computational and biostatistical work in this area includes:

- Genomic and phenotypic association analyses across ancestrally diverse
  subgroups
- Integration of electronic health record data with self-reported survey
  measures
- Survival analysis, mixed-effects modeling, and machine learning approaches
  to identify predictors of [health outcome]
- Replication of in vitro findings in observational cohort data

## Methods and Infrastructure

We are a hybrid wet and dry lab. Researchers have access to [list core
facilities or equipment available]. For computational work, we use R and
Python as primary analysis environments, with version-controlled workflows
hosted on GitHub. All of Us analyses are conducted within the Researcher
Workbench in compliance with data use agreements and IRB protocols.

Undergraduate researchers in our lab receive structured training in both
wet and computational methods regardless of their entry point. Students
joining through SOLUR are partnered with lab mentors to help complete independent projects or join onto previously established projects of interest.

## Research Training as a Scientific Value

Our lab participates in the SOLUR and SOLUR PREP models, which provide
structured, mentored research experiences with explicit attention to students
who have not had prior access to academic research environments. We are also
active advocates for the inclusion of online students as legitimate and
valued participants in undergraduate research, including within institutional
review processes, faculty mentorship structures, and conference presentation
opportunities.
